# Improvements, Applications & Recommendations: Salt and Ice Melting

## Scenario

A team investigated how the **amount of table salt** affects the **melting time** of ice cubes. They tested three levels: **0 g (control)**, **5 g**, and **10 g** of table salt sprinkled evenly over identical ice cubes. The ice cubes were made in the same tray and were approximately the same size. Each ice cube was placed in its own plastic cup at room temperature (~22 °C), the salt was added, and the team timed how long it took for the ice cube to fully melt. Five trials were run at each salt level.

---

## Results Summary

The team found that **more salt led to faster melting**. The control ice cubes (0 g salt) took an average of **18.4 minutes** to melt completely. Ice cubes with 5 g of salt melted in an average of **12.1 minutes**, and those with 10 g of salt melted in an average of **8.7 minutes**. Standard deviation was highest for the 0 g group (1.8 min) and lowest for the 10 g group (0.9 min).

The team noted that "fully melted" was sometimes hard to judge — a few tiny slivers of ice were hard to see in the salty water. They also observed that room temperature seemed to change slightly over the 2-hour testing period because the classroom door was opened and closed several times.
