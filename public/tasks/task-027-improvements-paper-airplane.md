# Improvements, Applications & Future Experiments: Paper Airplane Wing Shape

## Scenario

A team tested how **wing shape** (narrow, standard, wide) affects the **distance** a paper airplane flies. They threw each plane by hand down a school hallway and measured the landing distance with a tape measure. They ran 3 trials per wing shape.

## Setup Observations

- All planes were made from the same type of copy paper, but the wings were cut by eye rather than measured.
- A floor fan near one end of the hallway was left running.
- The tape measure was 10 m long, so one long flight had to be estimated.

## Procedure Observations

- Different team members threw different planes.
- Some throws were level and others were angled upward.
- Crease sharpness varied between planes, and a paper-clip nose weight was added to some planes but not others.

## Results Observations

| Wing shape | Trial 1 (m) | Trial 2 (m) | Trial 3 (m) | Average (m) |
|------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Narrow     | 4.2         | 5.1         | 3.8         | 4.4         |
| Standard   | 6.0         | 5.5         | 7.2         | 6.2         |
| Wide       | 3.9         | 6.8         | 4.1         | 4.9         |

Standard wings glided most smoothly. Wide-wing planes were unstable and sometimes stalled or spun.
