# Improvements, Applications & Future Experiments: Salt and Ice Melting

## Scenario

A team tested how the **amount of table salt** (0 g, 5 g, 10 g) affects the **time for an ice cube to melt**. Each ice cube sat in its own plastic cup at room temperature while the team timed how long it took to melt completely. They ran 3 trials per salt amount.

## Setup Observations

- Ice cubes came from the same tray, but their sizes were judged by eye and some looked slightly larger.
- The salt was sprinkled on by hand and not weighed again after being scooped.
- The cups were set on a desk near a window and a classroom door.

## Procedure Observations

- Two team members used separate stopwatches, and each decided on their own when the ice was "fully melted."
- Tiny slivers of ice were hard to see in the salty water.
- The classroom door was opened and closed several times during the 2-hour testing period.

## Results Observations

| Salt (g) | Trial 1 (min) | Trial 2 (min) | Trial 3 (min) | Average (min) |
|----------|---------------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| 0        | 17.0          | 20.5          | 17.7          | 18.4          |
| 5        | 11.2          | 13.4          | 11.7          | 12.1          |
| 10       | 8.3           | 9.4           | 8.4           | 8.7           |

More salt led to faster melting. The 0 g trials varied the most.
