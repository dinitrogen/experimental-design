# Qualitative Observations Practice: Baking Soda Reactions

## Scenario

A team investigated how the **type of liquid** affects the **reaction intensity** when mixed with baking soda. They tested three liquids: **water**, **lemon juice**, and **white vinegar**. For each liquid, they added 1 teaspoon of baking soda to 100 mL of liquid in a clear plastic cup and observed the reaction. They ran 5 trials for each liquid.

---

## Experiment Description

The team set up their workspace on a plastic tray lined with paper towels. They had three clear plastic cups per trial, a box of baking soda, a teaspoon, a graduated cylinder (for measuring 100 mL), and the three liquids in labeled bottles. They also had a ruler for measuring foam height and a stopwatch for timing.

Before starting, the **lemon juice** looked **pale yellow and slightly cloudy** with a **strong citrus smell** that the whole team noticed. The **white vinegar** was **completely clear** but had a **sharp, pungent smell** that made one team member wrinkle their nose. The **water** had **no color or smell**. The baking soda was a **fine white powder** that puffed slightly when scooped.

When they added baking soda to the **water**, there was a **quiet fizz** that lasted about 2 seconds. The powder **sank to the bottom** and slowly dissolved, leaving the water **slightly cloudy**. A few tiny bubbles rose to the surface. The cup felt **slightly cool** to the touch. After stirring, the water became **mostly clear** again with a thin gritty layer at the bottom.

When they added baking soda to the **lemon juice**, there was an **immediate fizzing eruption**. The mixture **foamed up rapidly** — a white bubbly foam rose about **3–4 cm** above the liquid line. The fizzing was **audible from across the table**, like a carbonated drink being opened. The foam had a **light, airy texture** and slowly collapsed over about 30 seconds. The lemon juice turned from pale yellow to an **opaque, milky white-yellow** color. The cup felt **noticeably cool** afterward. A faint **lemony smell mixed with something mineral-like** was noticeable.

When they added baking soda to the **vinegar**, the reaction was the **most vigorous**. The mixture **erupted with a loud hiss** and foam shot up **5–6 cm** above the liquid line — one trial nearly overflowed the cup. The foam was **thick and dense** compared to the lemon juice foam, and it persisted longer, taking about 45 seconds to settle. The **sharp vinegar smell intensified** during the reaction. The liquid went from clear to a **bubbly, slightly cloudy** appearance. The cup felt **cool** to the touch. Tiny droplets of foam **splattered onto the tray** during the most vigorous trials.

Between trials, the team rinsed cups and dried them with paper towels. They noticed the paper towels near the vinegar trials **smelled strongly** even after cleanup. On Trial 3 with lemon juice, a small seed from the juice **clogged the graduated cylinder briefly** before being poked through with a pencil.
