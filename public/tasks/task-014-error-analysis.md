# Error Analysis Practice

For each experiment below, identify **two errors** that could affect the results. For each error, state:
- The **type** of error (systematic, procedural or random)
- A **specific description** of the error
- The **specific effect** on the results (how does this error change the data?)

---

## Scenario 1 — Candle Burn Time Under Different Jar Sizes

A team tested how the volume of a glass jar affects how long a birthday candle stays lit when covered. They placed a lit birthday candle on a flat ceramic tile, then covered it with an upside-down glass jar and timed how long the flame lasted. They tested three jar sizes: 250 mL, 500 mL, and 1000 mL, with 5 trials each.

The candle was re-lit between each trial using the same long-reach lighter. They noticed that after several trials, the candle had burned down noticeably and the wick was shorter. They pressed the jar rims firmly against the tile each time, but the largest jar had a small chip on the rim that they didn't notice until trial 4.

## Scenario 2 — Paper Airplane Distance by Number of Wing Folds

A team investigated how the number of additional wing folds affects the flight distance of a paper airplane. They made identical paper airplanes from the same brand of printer paper and added 1, 2, or 3 extra folds to the wings. The same team member threw all airplanes from behind a taped line in the hallway.

They always tested all five 1-fold airplanes first, then all five 2-fold, then all five 3-fold. By the end of testing, the thrower mentioned their arm was getting tired. They measured distance from the starting line to where the airplane first touched the floor.

## Scenario 3 — Tea Steeping Color Intensity

A team tested how water temperature affects how quickly tea darkens water. They used cold tap water (~10 °C), room-temperature water (~22 °C), and hot water (~70 °C), pouring 200 mL into identical clear plastic cups. They dipped one tea bag into each cup for exactly 2 minutes.

They rated the resulting color intensity on a scale from 1 (lightest) to 5 (darkest) by holding the cup against a white sheet of paper. Different team members rated the color for different trials. They used the same tea bag for all three temperatures, starting with cold water, then room temperature, then hot — rinsing it briefly between cups.
